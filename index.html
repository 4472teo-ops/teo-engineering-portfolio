01 / ABOUT ME

Curious about flight.
Driven to figure it out.

I’m Teo, a student at Walt Whitman High School in Bethesda, Maryland. I’m interested in aerospace engineering because I like both sides of a problem: understanding the idea and building something that puts it to the test.

As a student pilot, I want to understand why an aircraft behaves the way it does. A small change in balance or geometry can make a big difference. Working on my own glider made that lesson hard to miss.

I also founded and lead my school’s Aviation Club, compete in varsity wrestling, and build websites. Those experiences ask for different skills, but they all reward showing up, paying attention, and staying with a problem.

03 / ENGINEERING PROCESS

The next version
starts with a question.

My glider made the engineering process real. A crash meant a repair, but it also meant a new question: was the problem the balance, the launch, or the shape? I learned to make a small change and test again, instead of changing everything and guessing what helped.

Build → Test → Fail → Analyze → Modify → Retest → Improve
  1. 01

    Identify the problem

    Define what the design needs to do.

  2. 02

    Build an initial design

    Make an idea testable.

  3. 03

    Test

    See what happens outside the sketch.

  4. 04

    Observe failure

    Pay attention to how it goes wrong.

  5. 05

    Identify variables

    Separate balance, geometry, and launch.

  6. 06

    Make a controlled change

    Give each adjustment a fair test.

  7. 07

    Test again

    Check whether the change helped.

  8. 08

    Analyze results

    Compare the record, including setbacks.

  9. 09

    Improve the design

    Keep what works and revisit what does not.

  10. 10

    Reflect

    Carry the lesson into the next project.
