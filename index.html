AEROSPACE · INDIVIDUAL PROJECT

Glider Design & Flight Testing

Summary: I designed and built a glider independently, then tested how changes to its mass distribution, launch angle, stabilizer, and flaps affected flight performance. After several unsuccessful launches, I used the results to iterate on the design and increased the best recorded distance from 30 to 63.

My process

I started with a 30 cm fuselage and used aerospace simulation data to study angle of attack, stall angle, stall velocity, glide angle, and drag. Early tests produced distances of 29–30, so I adjusted the nose mass and launch angle before making smaller changes to the vertical stabilizer and adding flaps.

Solution & impact

The final design flew successfully and reached a best recorded distance of 63. That was a 33-unit increase, or 110% improvement over the initial 30-unit result.

AERODYNAMICS · CAD

Airfoil Design & Analysis

Summary: I created an aircraft airfoil and studied how its geometry affects aerodynamic performance. The project connected CAD modeling with research into lift, drag, airflow, and angle of attack.

My role

This was an individual design project. I researched aerodynamic principles, created the model, and connected theoretical concepts with an actual aircraft component.

Process & solution

I researched airfoil geometry and the relationship between airflow, lift, and drag. I then developed the airfoil model in CAD and evaluated the design using aerodynamic concepts from my research.

Impact & success metric

The design met the project goal by producing 1 completed airfoil CAD model and connecting its geometry to the aerodynamic concepts I researched. Success was measured by completing the model and explaining how its shape relates to lift, drag, airflow, and angle of attack.

RESEARCH · AEROSPACE

Space Debris & Orbital Engineering

Summary: I researched space debris and the engineering problems created by objects left in orbit. I studied the causes and effects of orbital debris and investigated engineering approaches for reducing its impact on spacecraft and future missions.

My role

I completed the research and presentation work, organizing technical information into a format that explained the problem, its consequences, and possible engineering responses.

Process

I began by identifying why debris accumulates in orbit and why even small objects can create problems for spacecraft. I then researched the technical challenges involved in tracking, avoiding, and reducing orbital debris.

Impact & success metric

The project met its goal by producing 1 completed aerospace research presentation that explained the problem, risks, and possible engineering responses. Success was measured by completing the research deliverable and communicating the topic clearly to an audience.
