AEROSPACE · RESEARCH

Space Debris &
Orbital Engineering

I researched one of the less visible problems in modern aviation and aerospace: what happens when human-made objects remain in orbit after their useful mission is over.

The problem

Space is not empty. Satellites, spent rocket stages, fragments from collisions, and other objects can remain in orbit. At orbital speeds, even relatively small debris can become a serious hazard to operating spacecraft.

My role

This was an individual research and communication project. I was responsible for finding and organizing the technical information, deciding what was most important to explain, and turning a complicated aerospace problem into a clear presentation.

My process

01 · Identify the problem

I started by looking at why orbital debris exists and why it is difficult to remove once it is in space.

02 · Understand the risk

I studied how debris can threaten spacecraft and why collisions can create even more debris.

03 · Explore solutions

I researched engineering approaches such as tracking, collision avoidance, and debris mitigation strategies.

Impact & success metric

1 completed research project

The project met its goal by producing a completed aerospace research presentation covering the causes, risks, and possible engineering responses to orbital debris. Success was measured by completing the research deliverable and clearly communicating the technical problem.

Personal connection

What interested me most was the engineering responsibility behind the problem. Aerospace engineering is not only about getting an aircraft or spacecraft into the air; it is also about thinking about what happens afterward and designing systems that are safer and more sustainable.

Tools

Technical research · Presentation design · Aerospace concepts · Technical communication · Data/visual organization
